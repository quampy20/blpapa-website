What we practice

Filipino traditions, taught and danced in Elk Grove

Our Performing Arts Program teaches traditional Filipino folk dance, song, and instruments. Here is some of what that means, for anyone meeting it for the first time.

Tinikling

Two people clap a pair of bamboo poles together in rhythm while dancers step between them. It is named after the tikling bird and its way of stepping through grass traps. It is the dance most people picture when they picture Filipino folk dance, and it is harder than it looks.

Bayanihan

The old image of a whole neighbourhood lifting a bamboo house onto their shoulders and carrying it to a new spot. It has come to mean the habit of showing up for each other. It is why the festival is called what it is called.

Bukang Liwayway

Daybreak. First light. Our name, and the reason the sunrise sits at the centre of our logo. It is what a new organization full of pangarap, dreams, decided to call itself.

More is coming

Language, history, cooking, and instrument classes are on the way as our Educational Program grows. If you would like to be told when they open, send us a note and we will let you know.
